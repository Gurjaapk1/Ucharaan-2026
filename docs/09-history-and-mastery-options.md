## Three coherent routes

| Direction | Child's motivation and loop | What it offers | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. History expedition | Choose a historical encounter, read or hear a story, practise the next pauri, collect a journal entry | Strong discovery, place-based chapters and branching story choices | Highest reviewed-content burden; two progress tracks could confuse |
| B. Pauri mastery map | Pick a pauri, listen and recite each line, review stars, continue or replay | Clearest pronunciation loop and visible progress | History feels secondary unless deliberately integrated |
| C. Story + mastery | Optional short encounter, resume pauri, practise lines, review and move forward | Meaningful context with a predictable practice path | Needs restraint so the home screen does not become two competing apps |

Recommend C with B's replayable scorecard. This is a recommendation, not a team decision. The Paper screens deliberately retain the existing botanical visual language so the comparison focuses on experience structure.

## Make Oregon Trail an exploration model

Borrow chapter discovery, a travel journal and choices about which story to explore. The child is a learner exploring reviewed context. A choice can reveal a different story or reflection without changing a historical outcome. The practice path remains in its approved sequence while exploration can branch.

Do not carry over survival failure, resource starvation, combat or fictional decisions made on behalf of Guru Nanak. Do not turn Guru Nanak into an unlockable or equipped avatar. A learner avatar, landscape or journal keepsake is a separate proposal for personalization.

For Mario-like structure, use readable learning worlds and stage nodes. Worlds are app organization, not claims about the place a pauri was composed. Completing practice opens the next practice stage; three stars invite replay rather than block access. Avoid a timer or lives system that makes careful recitation feel rushed.

## Historical content boundaries

Japji Sahib has 38 pauris with Mool Mantar, an introductory salok and a concluding salok. The practice structure must account for opening and closing material. The date and manner of composition are disputed in the scholarly reference, so the design cannot present successive pauris as written at successive travel stops. [Punjabi University, Japu](https://eos.learnpunjabi.org/JAPU.html)

Use: “Practice Japji Sahib and discover stories from Guru Nanak's life and journeys.” The map is the learner's route. It is not a reconstruction of composition locations. Each historical card needs its source, educator reviewer and review status. Name a sakhi tradition where relevant rather than silently blending accounts.

Kartarpur is a candidate encounter because of its place in Guru Nanak's later life. The mockups show a story entry point, not a finished or approved historical lesson. [SGPC, Guru Nanak Sahib](https://new.sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-nanak-sahib/)

## Decisions to make independently

| Decision | Options | Recommendation and unresolved work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Star meaning | Numeric score bands; activity milestones; expert-reviewed mastery rubric | Reviewed rubric. Activity stars are simpler but must not be called mastery. Numeric cutoffs need calibration, not arbitrary percentages. |
| Advancement | Three stars required; finish then revisit; any pauri accessible | Finish then revisit for the guided path; review free navigation separately. |
| Rewards | Stars only; stars plus journal keepsakes; stars plus spendable coins | Stars plus keepsakes. Keep earned stars permanently; do not spend them. Currency reopens the earlier economy proposal. |
| Feedback timing | Every line; pauri end; end summary plus immediate recording help | End summary with immediate help for silence, microphone permission and failed capture. This interprets the transcript's scorecard discussion. |
| Historical depth | Mandatory quests; optional short encounters; separate library | Optional encounters for V1. Approve one before expanding. |

One possible rubric for discussion is supported recitation, demonstrated accuracy, then consistent accuracy on another attempt. These are candidate dimensions only, not implemented star definitions. An ustaad must define the evidence and acceptable variation. Uncertain audio should produce a retry or unscored state, not a misleading low pronunciation score. A replay retains the best demonstrated stars while reporting the current attempt separately.

The scorecard's two stars and suggested lines 2 and 5 are illustrative data. No model produced those judgments. Six lines is the proposed Pauri 1 segmentation, subject to content review. ## Proposed V1 journey

1. Choose Japji Sahib from the bani library.
2. See the current pauri and an optional historical encounter.
3. Open the story or resume practice directly.
4. Listen to and record each line; save position for resumption.
5. Review the completed pauri and targeted lines to revisit.
6. Continue at a nonperfect result or replay selected lines.
7. Keep earned progress and journal entries across sessions.

The demo should implement one complete practice unit and one approved historical encounter. Other banis and pauris remain explicitly marked previews. No new app code, scoring engine or historical curriculum is implemented by these mockups.

## Brainstorming and selection

Have the team pick A, B or C first, then choose each mechanic independently. Ask children to find their next practice, explain what a star means, return to a weak line and distinguish a story from a practice stage. Compare whether the history increases curiosity without hiding the learning task. Ask the educator whether the story and scoring feedback are accurate and appropriate. Use those observations to select the final flow before building broader content.

The prior product documents remain the baseline proposal. This options note reopens rewards, feedback timing and journey structure; it does not silently approve replacements. Parent setup, privacy and recording recovery from that baseline still apply.
