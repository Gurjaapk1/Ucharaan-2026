# Khalis Foundation challenge brief

Source: challenge text pasted by Gurasees on October 16, 2026. This is a structured summary, not an independently verified statement from Khalis. [Context index](README.md).

## Why this project exists

The challenge describes a gap between attention-capturing apps and the slow, daily attention required for Nitnem. Children may face a wall of Gurmukhi without help pronouncing it. Parents want correct uchaaran and a lasting relationship with Gurbani, but may lack access to an ustaad or time for daily supervision.

The brief proposes Kids Mode in Sundar Gutka, enabled by a parent, beginning with children and young adults and potentially serving other ages later. It claims 2.4 million annual users; that number has not been verified in this work.

## What the challenge asks teams to build

- An Uchaaran Helper using an ustaad's recordings of words and panktis plus pronunciation rules written in English. The ustaad sets the standard; AI measures against it.
- Word-level pronunciation feedback with a score and a specific weak word to practise. Generic Punjabi transcription and spelling comparison alone do not meet the requirement.
- Real-time word-aligned paath audio and Gurmukhi text, including tap-along highlights and missed/wrong-word underlines.
- Independent recitation with voice-following highlights and words marked for review.
- Streaks, banis completed, words mastered, and a short parent summary. Rewards must follow attention to Gurbani, never time spent in the app.
- Parent-enabled Kids Mode and recordings retained on-device unless the parent chooses otherwise.
- Validation against the ustaad's judgments and manually marked word timings.

The brief calls out Gurbani-specific pronunciation rules, including terminal sihari or aunkar that may not be spoken. This is context for the need for expert guidance, not a universal rule to encode without the ustaad's approved examples.

## Inputs and submission

The promised starting materials are Nitnem text through BaniDB, Japji Sahib paath audio, and an ustaad's recordings of selected words and panktis. Only sample BaniDB text has been fetched and verified in this work.

The requested five-minute submission shows a parent enabling Kids Mode, a child tapping along to a single pauri of Japji Sahib, reciting a pankti, receiving a score and exact review word, and a comparison against the ustaad's judgment. Judges focus on pronunciation measurement and word alignment. Streaks, parent summary, and visual polish can be simple.

The brief requests MIT licensing for code teams write for Khalis Foundation. This is a requirement, not a claim that a LICENSE file or all asset permissions have already been established.

## References included in the supplied brief

These references are preserved as source context; their claims were not independently researched in this project session.

- [UC Irvine attention article](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/how-sharpen-your-attention-and-meet-your-goals-2024): the brief cites a decline in average single-screen attention from about 150 to 47 seconds.
- [Pediatrics, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9923845): the brief describes short-term attention and self-control differences after viewing fast-paced television.
- [JAMA, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.8931): the brief describes an association between frequent digital media use and later ADHD symptoms in a teen cohort. This does not establish that this app will improve attention or prevent symptoms.
