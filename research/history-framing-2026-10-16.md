# Historical framing for the Japji Sahib journey

Checked October 16, 2026. This is a bounded design research note using original institutional publications. It is not a scholarly reconstruction of Guru Nanak's travel chronology. The Encyclopaedia of Sikhism entries are scholarly reference articles hosted by Punjabi University, not contemporary eyewitness documents.

## Findings that affect the design

| Question | Finding | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many Japji Sahib stages? | The text comprises Mool Mantar, an introductory salok, 38 pauris and a concluding salok. [Punjabi University, Japu](https://eos.learnpunjabi.org/JAPU.html) | A map can show 38 pauri milestones, but must include the opening and closing material. Pauri counts and app lesson counts are different. |
| Can each pauri be placed at a point in Guru Nanak's travels? | The Japu entry explicitly reports disagreement about the date and manner of composition. It describes a later Kartarpur arrangement as conjecture and an earlier River Bein account as a tradition. [Punjabi University, Japu](https://eos.learnpunjabi.org/JAPU.html) | These sources do not establish a pauri-by-pauri travel itinerary. Do not describe the map as the places where successive pauris were written. |
| Can the journey include Guru Nanak's life and travels? | SGPC's account describes Sultanpur Lodhi, the udasis and later settlement at Kartarpur. It calls the four major tours Char Udasis. [SGPC, Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji](https://new.sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-nanak-sahib/) | These provide possible historical chapters. Present a source-approved selection rather than implying a precisely verified route or attaching each location to a composition date. |
| How should sakhis be presented? | Janam-sakhi traditions contain narratives and interpretive discourses and developed in several forms. The university entry distinguishes these traditions and their transmission. [Punjabi University, Janam Sakhi](https://eos.learnpunjabi.org/JANAM%20SAKHI%20.html) | Name the tradition or source on each story card. Review the specific story before authoring dialogue or assigning it to a place. |
| Is Anand Sahib 41 pauris? | The Anandu entry identifies Guru Amar Das as its author and counts forty stanzas. [Punjabi University, Anandu](https://www.learnpunjabi.org/eos/ANANDU.html) | Correct the brainstorm's 41 to 40 if discussing a later Anand Sahib expansion. |

## Recommended wording

"Practice Japji Sahib and discover stories from Guru Nanak's life and journeys."

For a map note, use: "This is your learning route. It is not a reconstruction of where each pauri was composed."

For a Kartarpur card, a modest source-backed starting point is: "Guru Nanak settled at Kartarpur, where the community gathered for kirtan and langar." This paraphrases the [SGPC account](https://new.sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-nanak-sahib/). It still needs the project's content reviewer to approve the child-facing wording and illustration.

Avoid: "Travel with Guru Nanak as he writes each pauri of Japji Sahib." That implies chronology and locations that the reviewed sources do not establish.

## Separate the content types

- Source-backed context: authorship, textual structure and carefully attributed life context. Each card keeps its source and reviewer.
- Tradition: a named sakhi account, told with its provenance. Variation is handled in adult source notes when relevant rather than silently blended into a single historical claim.
- Design fiction: the learner's map, branching roads, fictional learner avatar, collectible stars, progress gates and invented nonhistorical challenges. These are app mechanisms and must not be attributed to Guru Nanak's life.

## Options for the product team

1. A pauri-first map with optional history stops. The learner progresses through the bani in its approved sequence. Historical cards appear between selected lessons as separate context. This makes the fewest historical assumptions and fits the existing pronunciation focus.
2. A history-first expedition with a separate practice track. Children explore reviewed locations and stories while a persistent Japji Sahib path records their ordered practice. Each location explicitly shows the next practice assignment without claiming that the pauri originated there. This adds content and navigation work.
3. Thematic learning worlds. Landscapes and paths organize practice, with reviewed history available in a journal. Worlds are visibly learning spaces rather than a literal sixteenth-century map. This allows the strongest Mario-like visual structure with the least implied historical precision.

All three can use three-star practice progress. Stars describe the learner's demonstrated practice milestones, not spiritual worth or a score assigned to the bani. The exact pronunciation criteria need ustaad approval and scoring validation. This is a design recommendation, not a historical finding.

## Before production content

Choose a Sikh educator or historical reviewer, the specific sakhi source tradition, approved place labels, and illustration conventions. Confirm the exact Gurmukhi source and lesson segmentation separately. This note does not verify maps, travel distances, dates for every stop, quotations, translations, or a bani text dataset.
